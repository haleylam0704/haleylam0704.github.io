I'm a Master's student in Psychology at Wake Forest University, working with [Dr. Mike Furr](https://furrrm.sites.wfu.edu/).

My interests are in the **psychology of human-AI interaction**. Specifically, they fall into three broad areas:

1. The psychological effects of human-AI interactions (both single interactions or sustained relationships) on users' wellbeing, loneliness, and existing human-human relationships.
2. How human-AI interactions affect beliefs and intellectual virtues (could they lead to better thinking?).
3. Individual differences that change who is disposed to interact with AI (heterogeneous dispositions) and who is harmed/benefited by human-AI interactions (heterogeneous effects).

Overall, my interests are in how (and for whom) AI chatbots psychologically affect well-being, relationships, and beliefs.

Additionally, I'm interested in societal **pluralism** and how individuals can interact across deep worldview differences to understand each other's views better. I'm currently involved in developing a scale for "relational pluralism," which assesses individual differences in engaging across differences with the motivation to increase mutual understanding.

## Current Research

I'm currently working on the following projects:

1. How accurately humans (compared to LLMs) can predict human behavior (Master's Thesis)
2. How to increase intellectual virtues (e.g., curiosity, open-mindedness, intellectual rigor) using LLMs
3. A theory paper on "relational pluralism" (engagement across worldview differences with the goal of increasing mutual understanding)
